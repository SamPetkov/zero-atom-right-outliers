\section{Marked extension of the invertible-weight proof}

The source proof \cite{BenArousEtAl2025} separates a fixed-dimensional row
mark from an independent high-dimensional Gaussian block. Conditioning on
activity changes only the joint law of the fixed-dimensional mark $(T,V)$.
We import four precise inputs from the source after this separation:
Proposition~2.7 for the weighted-Wishart bulk, Proposition~2.8 for absence
of weighted-Wishart outliers, Theorem~2.12 for the anisotropic deterministic
equivalent, and Lemma~2.13 for locally uniform transform convergence.
Theorem~2.12 explicitly allows random test vectors independent of the
Gaussian block. The source bulk theorem needs no inverse, whereas its
outlier theorem assumes nondegeneracy near zero; its ReLU discussion
identifies the uses of $D^{-1}$ as the obstruction.

The next proposition is therefore not a black-box application of the source
outlier theorem to a conditionally Gaussian mixture--the active sample is not
such a mixture. It is a reconstruction of the source argument from those
weighted-Wishart inputs, with every remaining step written for the exact joint
mark law.

\begin{proposition}[Scaled weighted-Wishart input from the source]
\label{S:source-input}
Let $p,m\to\infty$ with
\begin{equation}
\beta_d:=\frac mp\longrightarrow\beta\in(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
Let $X\in\R^{p\times m}$ have independent $N(0,1)$ entries, let
$D=\diag(T_1,\ldots,T_m)$ be independent of $X$, and assume that the
entries of $D$ are independent.  Suppose that, eventually,
$T_i\in[\tau_0,M_0]$ and that the empirical law
\begin{equation}
\widehat\rho_d=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\delta_{T_i}
\end{equation}
and its support converge almost surely to a compact probability law
$\rho\subset[\tau_0,M_0]$, weakly and in Hausdorff distance, respectively.
Put
\begin{equation}
\overline X=\frac{X}{\sqrt p},
\qquad
C_d=\overline X D\overline X^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
and let $\widetilde s_d$ be the physical solution of
\begin{equation}
\frac1{\widetilde s_d(z)}
=-z+\beta_d\int
\frac{t}{1+\widetilde s_d(z)t}\,\widehat\rho_d(dt).
\label{S:source-input-fixed}
\end{equation}
Let $\widetilde\nu$ be the limiting measure obtained by replacing
$(\widehat\rho_d,\beta_d)$ with $(\rho,\beta)$.

The row-normalized forms of Propositions~2.7--2.8,
Theorem~2.12, and Lemma~2.13 of Ref.~\cite{BenArousEtAl2025} imply the
following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The empirical spectral distribution of $C_d$ converges weakly almost
surely to $\widetilde\nu$.
\item If $J\Subset\R\setminus\supp\widetilde\nu$ is bounded, then
\begin{equation}
\sigma(C_d)\cap J=\varnothing
\end{equation}
eventually almost surely.
\item For $z\notin\sigma(C_d)$, define the exact lower Schur block
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_d(z)
:=D-D\overline X^{\mathsf T}(C_d-zI)^{-1}\overline X D.
\label{S:source-exact-K}
\end{equation}
On every compact $K\Subset\C\setminus\supp\widetilde\nu$ with positive
distance from the support,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_d(z)
\ \sim_{\mathrm{aniso}}\
D\bigl(I+\widetilde s_d(z)D\bigr)^{-1}
\label{S:source-lower-block}
\end{equation}
locally uniformly: for any random unit vectors $u_d,v_d\in\R^m$ that may
depend on $D$ but are independent of $X$,
\begin{equation}
\sup_{z\in K}
\left|
 u_d^{\mathsf T}
 \left[\mathcal K_d(z)-D(I+\widetilde s_d(z)D)^{-1}\right]
 v_d
\right|\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
in probability, and in the source coupling eventually almost surely.
The source writes the corresponding lower inverse block with the opposite
sign; the definition \eqref{S:source-exact-K} absorbs that sign, so the
positive matrix on the right of \eqref{S:source-lower-block} is the object
that enters the Schur matrix \eqref{S:source-Q} below.
\item The functions $\widetilde s_d$ converge locally uniformly off the
limiting support to the physical transform $\widetilde s$ of
$\widetilde\nu$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For clarity about normalization, Proposition~\ref{S:source-input} is the
row-normalized form of the cited source results.  The source isolates an
orthogonal Gaussian block of row dimension $d-q$, writes its deterministic
self-consistent equation with aspect ratio $n/(d-q)$ in equation (2.11), and
approximates the lower linearization block by
$-D(I+\widetilde S^{(d)}D)^{-1}$ in equation (2.19).  Replacing
$(d-q,n)$ by $(p,m)$ and writing the normalized Gaussian matrix as
$\overline X=X/\sqrt p$ gives \eqref{S:source-input-fixed} and
\eqref{S:source-lower-block}.  Equivalently, one may start from the
sample-normalized matrix $m^{-1}XDX^{\mathsf T}$ and use the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \frac1pXDX^{\mathsf T}
 =\beta_d\left(\frac1mXDX^{\mathsf T}\right),
 \qquad \beta_d=\frac mp,
\end{equation}
together with the dilation formula
$S_{a\mu}(z)=a^{-1}S_\mu(z/a)$ and the corresponding block-resolvent
rescaling.  Both routes give the displayed equation and the same Schur block;
no finite-dimensional identification of $p$ with another dimension is made.
These are the only local-law, transform-convergence, bulk, and no-outlier
inputs used below.
