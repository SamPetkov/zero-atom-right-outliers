PROMPT USED FOR

“RIGHT-OUTLIER THEORY FOR SELF-COUPLED SAMPLE COVARIANCE
MATRICES WITH ZERO DIAGONAL WEIGHTS”


1. SOURCE AND SCOPE

This task attacks a precise special case of the following genuine open
problem listed on SolveAll:

Outlier theory beyond non-degeneracy/invertibility assumptions
(ReLU and zero diagonal entries)

https://solveall.org/problem/outlier-theory-beyond-non-degeneracyinvertibility-assumptions-relu-and-zero-diag

Primary source:

Gérard Ben Arous, Reza Gheissari, Jiaoyang Huang, and Aukosh Jagannath,

“Local geometry of high-dimensional mixture models:
Effective spectral theory and dynamical transitions”

https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15655

The source paper studies self-coupled random matrices of the form

    M_d = (1/n) A D A^T,

where:

- A is a high-dimensional Gaussian-mixture data matrix;
- D is diagonal;
- each diagonal entry of D may depend on the same data column appearing
  in A, through finitely many low-dimensional projections.

The paper proves:

1. a deterministic limiting bulk spectral law under boundedness and
   low-dimensional coupling assumptions; and

2. exact outlier and outlier-eigenvector equations when D is effectively
   invertible and does not accumulate mass near zero.

The paper explicitly states that ReLU-type activations are not covered
because the ReLU derivative is zero on a set of positive probability.
Consequently, D has exact zero entries, while the outlier proof uses D^{-1}
at several points.

This task isolates the cleanest first extension:

    D is nonnegative;
    D may have a positive-probability atom at zero;
    every nonzero entry of D is uniformly bounded away from zero.

This includes pure ReLU gates and ReLU-gated positive weights.

The task does not initially require treating:

- signed nonzero weights;
- positive weights accumulating continuously at zero;
- unbounded weights;
- growing-dimensional summary statistics;
- the full nonsmooth neural-network Hessian;
- training-data dependence along an entire SGD trajectory.

A complete solution to the exact theorem below counts as success.

Before claiming novelty, conduct a current literature search to determine
whether this zero-atom outlier theorem has been proved after the source paper.

Search at least:

- the exact SolveAll problem title;
- papers citing arXiv:2502.15655;
- singular diagonal weights in self-coupled sample covariance matrices;
- random covariance matrices with Bernoulli diagonal weights;
- outliers with an atom at zero in the variance distribution;
- spiked covariance matrices with missing observations;
- anisotropic local laws for singular weighted covariance matrices;
- ReLU Hessian outlier random matrix theory;
- gated Gaussian covariance matrices;
- truncated Gaussian sample covariance outliers;
- finite-rank deformations of singular sample covariance matrices;
- pseudoinverse linearization of sample covariance matrices;
- 2026 and later preprints.

Use primary sources. Do not infer novelty merely from the absence of a
result in one database.


2. CURRENT TASK STATEMENT


2.1 Fixed parameters

Fix:

    k,C,q in N,
    q = C+k,

    lambda > 0,
    phi > 0,

    p_1,...,p_k > 0,
    sum_{b=1}^k p_b = 1,

and a positive-semidefinite matrix

    G in R^{q x q}.

All of these quantities are fixed independently of d and n.

The ratio n/d satisfies

    n/d -> phi

as

    n,d -> infinity.


2.2 Geometric realization of G

For every sufficiently large d, let

    B_d =
    [x_1^{(d)},...,x_C^{(d)},
     mu_1^{(d)},...,mu_k^{(d)}]
    in R^{d x q}

satisfy

    B_d^T B_d = G.

Let

    L_d in R^{d x q}

have orthonormal columns and satisfy

    L_d^T B_d = sqrt(G),

where sqrt(G) is the unique positive-semidefinite square root.

If G is rank deficient, L_d may contain additional orthonormal directions
outside the span of B_d. All statements must remain valid in that case.

For hidden label b in {1,...,k}, define

    m_b = L_d^T mu_b^{(d)}.

Equivalently, m_b is the column of sqrt(G) corresponding to the class mean
mu_b.


2.3 Gaussian-mixture samples

For each ell=1,...,n, independently sample:

    b_ell ~ Categorical(p_1,...,p_k),

    g_ell ~ N(0,I_q),

    w_ell ~ N(0,I_{d-q}).

All labels and Gaussian variables are mutually independent.

Let L_d^perp be any orthonormal complement of L_d and define

    Y_ell
      =
    L_d(m_{b_ell}+lambda^{-1/2}g_ell)
      +
    lambda^{-1/2} L_d^perp w_ell.

Equivalently,

    Y_ell
      ~
    N(mu_{b_ell}^{(d)},lambda^{-1}I_d).

Let

    A_d = [Y_1 ... Y_n] in R^{d x n}.


2.4 Self-coupled zero-atom diagonal weights

For each hidden class b, let

    h_b : R^q -> [0,M]

be a measurable function, for some fixed finite M.

Define

    D_ell,ell = h_{b_ell}(g_ell).

Thus D is coupled to A through the same low-dimensional Gaussian vector
g_ell that determines the projection of Y_ell into the summary-statistic
space.

Assume the following zero-gap condition.

There exists a fixed tau>0 such that, almost surely,

    h_b(g) belongs to {0} union [tau,M]

for every b.

The probability

    pi_b = P(h_b(g)>0)

may lie anywhere in [0,1].

Define the overall active probability

    pi = sum_{b=1}^k p_b pi_b.

The central random matrix is

    M_d = (1/n) A_d D A_d^T.

It is real symmetric and positive semidefinite.


3. EFFECTIVE BULK AND OUTLIER EQUATIONS

The following definitions are adapted from Equations (1.9)–(1.13) of the
primary source.

Check every normalization and index convention against the source before
using them in a final proof.


3.1 Effective bulk

Let g~N(0,I_q).

For z in the upper half-plane, define S_G(z) as the Stieltjes-transform
solution of

                        k
    1+z S_G(z)
      =
    phi sum p_b E[
                        S_G(z) h_b(g)
                       -------------------------------
                       lambda phi+S_G(z)h_b(g)
                     ].
                       b=1

Choose the unique branch satisfying the defining properties of a Stieltjes
transform:

    Im S_G(z)>0 when Im z>0,

and

    z S_G(z) -> -1

as |z|->infinity in the upper half-plane.

Let

    nu_G

be the corresponding probability measure.

The atom h_b(g)=0 contributes zero inside the displayed fraction.

The source paper’s bulk theorem is intended to imply convergence of the
empirical spectral measure of M_d to nu_G even in the presence of the atom
at zero. This fact must be verified from the source theorem rather than
reproved from memory.

Write

    lambda_+(G) = sup supp(nu_G)

for the right edge of the effective bulk.


3.2 Effective outlier matrix

For b in {1,...,k}, define

    v_b(g) = lambda^{-1/2}g+m_b in R^q.

For real z outside supp(nu_G), define

    F_G(z)
      =
    lambda phi
    sum_{b=1}^k p_b
    E[
        h_b(g)
        --------------------------------
        lambda phi+S_G(z)h_b(g)

        v_b(g)v_b(g)^T
      ].

The proposed effective right outliers are the real roots

    z>lambda_+(G)

of

    det(z I_q-F_G(z))=0.

Count roots with their analytic multiplicities.

Define

    Z_G^+
      =
    {z>lambda_+(G):
     det(z I_q-F_G(z))=0}.


4. MAIN ZERO-ATOM OUTLIER CONJECTURE

Resolve the following theorem completely.


THEOREM TO PROVE

Under the setup above, the positive-probability zero entries of D do not
alter the effective right-outlier equations.

More precisely:


4.1 Outlier counting on compact intervals

Let

    I=[a,b]

be a compact interval satisfying

    lambda_+(G)<a<b<infinity,

and suppose neither endpoint is an effective root:

    det(aI_q-F_G(a)) != 0,
    det(bI_q-F_G(b)) != 0.

Then, with probability tending to one,

    number of eigenvalues of M_d in I
      =
    number of roots of det(zI_q-F_G(z)) in I,

with both sides counted with multiplicity.


4.2 No spurious right outliers

For every epsilon>0, there exists a deterministic K<infinity such that,
with probability tending to one, every eigenvalue of M_d satisfying

    eigenvalue > lambda_+(G)+epsilon

lies within o(1) of an element of Z_G^+ intersected with
[lambda_+(G)+epsilon,K].

In particular, exact zero entries of D do not generate additional positive
right outliers absent from the effective determinant equation.


4.3 Existence of empirical outliers

If z_* is an isolated effective root of multiplicity m and

    z_*>lambda_+(G),

then for every sufficiently small fixed delta>0, with probability tending
to one, M_d has exactly m eigenvalues in

    (z_*-delta,z_*+delta),

and all those eigenvalues converge to z_*.


4.4 Simple-root eigenvector equation

Suppose z_* is a simple root.

Let z_d be the corresponding empirical eigenvalue and let u_d be an
associated unit eigenvector.

Define its projection into the summary-statistic space by

    r_d = L_d^T u_d in R^q.

Prove that, after choosing a sign and passing to convergent subsequences
when necessary,

    (z_* I_q-F_G(z_*))r = 0,

and

    ||r||_2^2
      -
    <r,partial_z F_G(z_*)r>
      =
    1.

Equivalently, prove the approximate finite-dimensional equations

    ||(z_d I_q-F_G(z_d))r_d||_2 = o_P(1),

and

    |
      ||r_d||_2^2
      -
      <r_d,partial_zF_G(z_d)r_d>
      -
      1
    |
      =
    o_P(1).


4.5 Multiple roots

For a root of multiplicity greater than one, formulate and prove the
correct spectral-projection or invariant-subspace statement.

Do not state convergence of individual eigenvectors when the limiting
eigenspace has rotational ambiguity.


4.6 Exact kernel contribution

Let

    N_active
      =
    number of ell such that D_ell,ell>0.

Prove that, almost surely for each finite d,

    rank(M_d)=min(d,N_active),

provided pi>0.

Consequently,

    dim ker(M_d)
      =
    d-min(d,N_active)

almost surely, and

    (1/d)dim ker(M_d)
      ->
    (1-phi pi)_+

in probability, and preferably almost surely.

Reconcile this exact kernel formula with the atom of nu_G at zero.

The zero eigenvalues are not to be misclassified as outliers.


5. OPTIONAL STRONGER EXTENSIONS

Only pursue these after the main theorem is complete.


5.1 All intervals away from zero

Extend the outlier-count theorem to every compact interval

    I subset R \ (supp(nu_G) union {0}),

allowing signed nonzero weights

    h_b(g) in [-M,-tau] union {0} union [tau,M].


5.2 Converging summary statistics

Allow

    G_d -> G

rather than requiring B_d^T B_d=G exactly.

State and verify the analogue of the source paper’s Assumption 3, including
the discontinuity introduced by hard ReLU gates.


5.3 Nonzero weights approaching zero

Replace the fixed gap condition by a quantitative small-ball condition on
the nonzero part, while still permitting an atom at zero:

    P(0<h_b(g)<epsilon)
      <=
    a_epsilon,

where

    a_epsilon -> 0.

This is closer to general ReLU-gated Hessian and gradient weights.


5.4 Training trajectories

Establish a result uniform over a compact trajectory of summary statistics

    G_t,

so that the outlier locations can be followed along an effective SGD
dynamics.


6. RELU-GATE COROLLARY

At minimum, derive the following explicit corollary of the main theorem.

Suppose

    h_b(g)
      =
    a_b(g) 1{ell_b(g)>0},

where:

- ell_b(g) is a nonconstant affine function of g;
- a_b(g) is measurable;
- tau <= a_b(g) <= M whenever ell_b(g)>0.

Then D has a positive-probability atom at zero and satisfies the zero-gap
condition.

Prove that the complete right-outlier theorem applies.

The pure ReLU gate is

    h_b(g)=1{ell_b(g)>0}.

Explain exactly which empirical covariance, gradient, Gauss–Newton, or
neural-network block this represents.

Do not claim a theorem for a classical ReLU Hessian unless all
nondifferentiability and second-derivative issues have been handled
rigorously.

If a generalized Hessian is used, define it explicitly.


7. COMPLETE SUCCESS CONDITIONS

A successful resolution must include all of the following.


7.1 Rigorous zero-atom extension

Prove the outlier-counting theorem without assuming that D is invertible.

The proof must identify every place in the source argument where D^{-1} is
used and replace or bypass that use.


7.2 Correct deterministic equations

Prove that the same equations S_G and F_G remain correct when D has an
atom at zero.

If the correct equations differ, derive and prove the corrected equations.

Do not assume the desired formula merely because zero terms vanish
algebraically in the expectation.


7.3 Multiplicity

Preserve analytic and algebraic multiplicities.

A theorem locating one eigenvalue near every simple numerical root does not
establish the general result.


7.4 No spurious eigenvalues

Prove both directions:

- every effective root produces empirical outliers;
- every empirical right outlier corresponds to an effective root.


7.5 Eigenvectors

Prove the summary-space eigenvector equations or the correct
spectral-projection analogue.

An eigenvalue-only theorem is incomplete.


7.6 Exact zero-spectrum treatment

Separate:

- exact finite-dimensional zero eigenvalues;
- a limiting atom at zero;
- small positive bulk eigenvalues;
- positive right outliers.

Do not apply an inverse to a singular matrix.


7.7 Uniform probabilistic control

Every high-probability event must be uniform over the deterministic spectral
sets on which the proof needs uniformity.

Pointwise convergence of resolvents is not enough for eigenvalue counting.


7.8 Current literature audit

Search current primary literature before claiming novelty.

A result already proved after the source paper must be attributed rather
than relabeled as new.


8. RESULTS THAT DO NOT COUNT

None of the following is a complete solution:

- proving only the limiting empirical spectral distribution;
- reproving the bulk theorem already present in the source paper;
- treating D independent of A only;
- treating deterministic zero locations only;
- treating only Bernoulli thinning independent of the samples;
- treating only q=0;
- treating only one numerical choice of G;
- proving only a scalar q=1 example;
- numerical agreement between empirical eigenvalues and the determinant
  equation;
- replacing exact zero weights by a fixed epsilon and never taking
  epsilon to zero;
- taking epsilon to zero without a uniform-in-d argument;
- an operator-norm perturbation bound without root-count stability;
- a weak-convergence argument without control of support edges;
- locating empirical eigenvalues without ruling out spurious ones;
- proving only simple-root cases;
- ignoring multiplicity;
- assuming all roots remain a fixed distance from the bulk;
- excluding rank-deficient G without justification;
- using D^{-1} on the active range without controlling the inactive range;
- using a Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse without deriving the correct Schur
  complement;
- assuming conditioning on D>0 preserves an ordinary Gaussian-mixture law;
- replacing normalization by 1/N_active without tracking N_active/n;
- claiming a zero eigenvalue is an outlier;
- applying Davis–Kahan without an established spectral gap;
- using weak convergence of nu_epsilon to infer Hausdorff convergence of
  supports;
- using a numerical root of the Stieltjes equation without identifying
  the physical branch;
- proving only an upper bound on the number of right outliers;
- treating ReLU as twice continuously differentiable;
- a reduction ending in an unproved support-stability theorem comparable
  in difficulty to the target;
- a theorem whose constants depend on d;
- a literature summary without a proof.

11. INDEPENDENT APPROACH FAMILIES


11.1 Epsilon-regularization

Replace every zero weight by epsilon>0.

Apply the existing invertible-D theorem for fixed epsilon.

Prove:

1. operator-norm convergence M_{d,epsilon}->M_d as epsilon->0,
   uniformly for large d;

2. convergence of S_{G,epsilon} to S_G uniformly on compact subsets of the
   complement of supp(nu_G);

3. convergence of F_{G,epsilon} to F_G;

4. stability of the right bulk edge;

5. stability of determinant-root multiplicities;

6. transfer of empirical eigenvalue counts;

7. transfer of spectral projections and eigenvector equations.

The order of limits must be explicit.

A valid proof may use:

    choose epsilon small;
    then choose d sufficiently large;
    finally remove epsilon.

Do not silently interchange the two limits.


11.2 Active-column reduction

Let

    I_active={ell:D_ell,ell>0}.

Write

    M_d
      =
    (N_active/n)
    [
      (1/N_active)
      A_active D_active A_active^T
    ].

Conditional on activity, D_active is invertible.

Derive the exact conditional law of:

- hidden labels;
- low-dimensional Gaussian coordinates;
- orthogonal Gaussian coordinates;
- positive diagonal weights.

Determine whether the source proof extends to this conditional law.

Account exactly for the random factor

    N_active/n -> pi.

Do not claim that the active columns remain an ordinary Gaussian mixture;
the low-dimensional coordinates are generally truncated or reweighted.


11.3 Pseudoinverse linearization

Replace the source linearization involving D^{-1} by a block decomposition
of the sample indices into active and inactive coordinates.

Derive the exact determinant identity without taking an inverse on the zero
block.

Possible tools include:

- Moore–Penrose pseudoinverses;
- generalized Schur complements;
- restriction to ran(D);
- orthogonal projections;
- determinant identities on the active block.

Do not insert D^\dagger into the existing formula without proving that no
extra determinant factors or zero modes are lost.


11.4 Direct active-block Schur complement

After permuting samples, write

              [D_+  0]
    D       = [      ],
              [ 0   0]

and

    A=[A_+ A_0].

Then

    A D A^T=A_+D_+A_+^T.

Linearize only the active matrix.

Track the fact that A_+ is selected using information contained in its own
columns.

Seek a conditional anisotropic local law for the orthogonal Gaussian part.


11.5 Direct resolvent comparison

Compare

    R_epsilon(z)
      =
    (M_{d,epsilon}-zI)^{-1}

and

    R_0(z)
      =
    (M_d-zI)^{-1}

using the resolvent identity.

For z a fixed distance from the spectrum, prove

    ||R_epsilon(z)-R_0(z)||
      <=
    C(z) epsilon.

Lift this to the finite-dimensional projected resolvents used in the
outlier determinant.


11.6 Analytic determinant and Rouché theory

Let

    q_epsilon(z)
      =
    det(zI_q-F_{G,epsilon}(z)),

and

    q_0(z)
      =
    det(zI_q-F_G(z)).

Prove uniform convergence on contours that stay away from the limiting bulk.

Apply Rouché’s theorem or the argument principle to preserve root counts.

Treat:

- multiple roots;
- roots close to one another;
- roots approaching the bulk edge;
- contours enclosing no support points.


11.7 Support-edge stability

Prove the required form of

    lambda_+(G,epsilon)
      ->
    lambda_+(G,0).

Possible routes:

- deterministic fixed-point analysis;
- subordination functions;
- free multiplicative convolution;
- finite-matrix operator-norm comparison plus known edge convergence for
  epsilon>0;
- variational characterization of the support edge.

Weak convergence alone is insufficient.


11.8 Free-probability formulation

The limiting bulk is a multiplicative free convolution between the weight
law and a Marchenko–Pastur law, with the correct scaling.

Study continuity of this convolution when

    rho_epsilon
      ->
    rho_0

and rho_0 has an atom at zero.

Determine the exact zero-atom formula and right-edge continuity.


11.9 Finite-rank deformation viewpoint

Decompose A into:

- the q-dimensional summary-statistic component;
- the independent orthogonal Gaussian component.

Treat the summary component as a finite-rank deformation of a weighted
sample covariance bulk.

Adapt standard outlier theory to a singular weight distribution.

Identify precisely which finite-rank deformation theorem applies and whether
its hypotheses permit an atom at zero.


11.10 Direct anisotropic local law

Prove an anisotropic deterministic equivalent for the singular-weight
linearization directly.

Construct a stable matrix Dyson equation that does not require D^{-1}.

This route may be technically stronger than necessary; mark it blocked if it
requires reproving a full optimal local law without additional payoff.


11.11 Spectral projection method

Use contour integrals

    P_d
      =
    -(1/(2 pi i))
    integral_Gamma (M_d-zI)^{-1} dz

to transfer outlier eigenspaces from epsilon>0 to epsilon=0.

This may be preferable to individual Davis–Kahan arguments for multiple
roots.


11.12 Counterexample search

Search for examples where the unchanged determinant equation fails.

Potential failure mechanisms include:

- the active fraction pi is below 1/phi;
- the zero atom creates a new hard edge;
- an effective root converges to zero;
- conditioning on activity alters a low-rank term omitted from F_G;
- regularized roots fail to converge;
- multiple components of the bulk merge as epsilon->0;
- a positive eigenvalue generated by the active/inactive boundary is not
  represented by F_G.

Any apparent counterexample must be verified at increasing d and, where
possible, converted into an exact asymptotic calculation.


12. DETAILED REGULARIZATION PROGRAM TO TEST

The following is a candidate proof route, not an established proof.


12.1 Regularized matrices

For epsilon>0 define

    D_epsilon
      =
    D+epsilon P_0,

and

    M_{d,epsilon}
      =
    (1/n) A D_epsilon A^T.


12.2 Apply the known theorem

For each fixed epsilon, verify the source assumptions and obtain:

- bulk nu_{G,epsilon};
- right edge lambda_+(G,epsilon);
- effective matrix F_{G,epsilon};
- exact outlier counting away from the bulk;
- eigenvector equations.


12.3 Matrix perturbation

Establish a high-probability event on which

    sup_{0<epsilon<=epsilon_0}
    ||M_{d,epsilon}-M_d||
      <=
    C epsilon.

The constant C must not depend on d.


12.4 Deterministic convergence

Prove, on every compact set K separated from supp(nu_G),

    sup_{z in K}
    |S_{G,epsilon}(z)-S_G(z)|
      ->
    0,

and

    sup_{z in K}
    ||F_{G,epsilon}(z)-F_G(z)||
      ->
    0.


12.5 Root stability

For a contour Gamma that encloses a chosen collection of effective roots
but no part of supp(nu_G), prove that for sufficiently small epsilon,

    q_epsilon

and

    q_0

have the same number of zeros inside Gamma.


12.6 Empirical transfer

Use the known epsilon theorem and Weyl or spectral-projection perturbation
to transfer the eigenvalue count from M_{d,epsilon} to M_d.

The proof must quantify the gap between:

- Gamma or I;
- the bulk;
- neighboring roots;
- and zero.


12.7 Remove epsilon

Complete a correct quantifier argument of the form:

For every desired error probability eta and every fixed admissible interval
I:

1. choose epsilon sufficiently small using deterministic stability;
2. choose d sufficiently large using the fixed-epsilon theorem;
3. transfer the result to epsilon=0;
4. conclude the desired probability tends to one.

Do not choose epsilon=epsilon_d unless all theorems used are uniform along
that sequence.

14. ADVERSARIAL AUDIT

Every candidate proof must be checked independently.

Locate the first unsupported inference rather than initially attempting to
repair it.

Audit specifically for the following errors.


14.1 Invalid inverse

Any occurrence of D^{-1} when D has zero entries is invalid.

Check whether the proof actually means:

- restriction to ran(D);
- inversion of D_+;
- or a Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.


14.2 Incorrect conditioning

Conditioning on D_ell,ell>0 changes the distribution of the low-dimensional
Gaussian vector g_ell.

Do not replace active samples by the original Gaussian mixture.


14.3 Lost normalization

The matrix is normalized by n, not by N_active.

The factor N_active/n must be retained.


14.4 Zero atom versus zero eigenvalues

Distinguish:

- P(D=0);
- the limiting zero mass of nu_G;
- exact finite-dimensional nullity;
- a possible hard edge at zero.


14.5 Weak support reasoning

Weak convergence of probability measures does not imply convergence of
support edges.

An explicit support-stability argument is required.


14.6 Nonuniform regularization

A theorem valid for each fixed epsilon does not automatically hold for a
sequence epsilon_d->0.


14.7 Incorrect Weyl transfer

Weyl’s inequality controls ordered eigenvalue displacement.

It does not by itself prove analytic multiplicity, eigenvector convergence,
or root-count equality when gaps vanish.


14.8 Bulk-edge roots

A root located exactly at lambda_+(G) is not a separated outlier.

Do not claim an outlier theorem at a BBP transition without a separate
critical analysis.


14.9 Multiple roots

Davis–Kahan for an individual eigenvector is inappropriate when the
limiting eigenvalue has multiplicity greater than one.


14.10 Rank-deficient G

Do not assume G is invertible unless explicitly stated.

The summary-statistic subspace may have dimension smaller than q.


14.11 Stieltjes branch

Algebraic fixed-point equations may have multiple solutions.

Only the physical Stieltjes branch defines nu_G.


14.12 Hidden poles

Before using analytic determinant arguments, verify that

    lambda phi+S_G(z)h_b(g)

does not vanish on the contour with positive probability.


14.13 ReLU differentiability

ReLU is not C^2.

Do not import a smooth-Hessian theorem without defining the object under
study and proving that the relevant derivatives exist almost surely.


14.14 False independence

D_ell,ell and Y_ellY_ell^T are generally dependent.

Symmetry may produce independence in special centered examples only.


14.15 Root convergence without compactness

Uniform convergence on compact sets does not control roots escaping to
infinity.

Supply an operator-norm or determinant bound establishing a finite search
region.


14.16 Literature mismatch

A theorem for:

- independent diagonal weights;
- deterministic population covariance;
- ordinary missing-at-random data;
- or separable covariance matrices

does not automatically solve the self-coupled problem.
